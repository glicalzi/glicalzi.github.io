I'm a PhD student at the Institute for the Interdisciplinary Study of Language Evolution (University of Zurich). Previously, I completed a BSc in Experimental Linguistics at University College London (Faculty of Brain Sciences) and an MSc in Cognitive Neuroscience at the University of Trento (CIMeC) with a year abroad at the Max Planck Institute for Psycholinguistics (Psychology of Language).

My research combines naturalistic neuroimaging paradigms (fMRI, sEEG) and deep neural networks to understand the neural correlates of speech production planning, with a particular focus on spontaneous speech production. In current work, we’re leveraging large language models to better understand the earliest stages of speech production planning.

**Check out my latest work here:**
[Li Calzi, G., Meyer, A. S., & van der Burght, C. L. (2025). The time course of phonological encoding: Insights from time-resolved MVPA. *Journal of Neuroscience*, *45*(41), e0546252025.](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.0546-25.2025)
